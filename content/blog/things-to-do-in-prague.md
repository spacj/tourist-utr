Prague feels like a living museum, where Gothic spires sit beside Art Nouveau façades and the river cuts a steady line through the city centre. That's why the things to do in Prague can be tackled on foot – most highlights sit within a comfortable stroll, turning a single‑day visit into a compact adventure rather than a race. Grab comfortable shoes and a light raincoat – the cobbles are ready for you.

## Things to do in Prague: historic core

The historic core is where most first‑time visitors start, and for good reason. Old Town Square frames the **Astronomical Clock**, which puts on a brief mechanical show each hour. A short walk away, the twin spires of Týn Church dominate the square.

From there, cross the **Charles Bridge**. Early morning offers the quietest atmosphere, with the statues seeming to watch the river alone. Take a moment on the bridge to read the plaques that explain each saint’s story.

**Key stops**:
- Old Town Square and Astronomical Clock
- Týn Church
- Charles Bridge
- Lesser Town (Malá Strana) at the bridge’s southern end

## Royal Castle and its surroundings

Perched above the city, the Prague Castle complex crowns the skyline. Inside, **St. Vitus Cathedral** showcases a Gothic interior that rewards the short climb up its stairs. Nearby, **Golden Lane** lines a narrow street with tiny, colourful houses once home to royal guards.

A short walk brings you to the **Royal Garden**, a neatly trimmed space offering a quiet view over the city roofs. You can also catch the Changing of the Guard at the main gate – a simple, free spectacle.

**Key stops**:
- St. Vitus Cathedral
- Golden Lane
- Royal Garden
- The Castle’s outer courtyards

## Jewish Quarter and Old Town

The former Jewish Quarter, Josefov, nestles just north of Old Town Square. Its narrow lanes lead to historic synagogues, notably the **Old‑New Synagogue**, the oldest active synagogue in Europe, and the **Pinkas Synagogue**, whose walls bear the names of Czech Jews lost in the Holocaust.

A short walk returns you to the **Powder Tower**, one of the original city gates, and the **Church of Our Lady before Týn**, its spires dominating the skyline. Small courtyards here often host street performances.

**Key stops**:
- Old‑New Synagogue
- Pinkas Synagogue
- Powder Tower
- Church of Our Lady before Týn

## Riverfront walks and bridges

The Vltava River offers a natural promenade linking the city’s main districts. Starting at the **Charles Bridge**, head north along the embankment to the **Mánes Bridge**, where contemporary sculptures punctuate the walk.

Cross to the left bank and let **Lesser Town** unfold with its baroque palaces and the ever‑changing **John Lennon Wall**, a canvas of graffiti and peace messages. A short detour to **Kampa Island** rewards you with quiet parkland, a modern art museum, and a gentle curve of the river.

**Key stops**:
- Charles Bridge
- Mánes Bridge
- John Lennon Wall
- Kampa Island

## Parks, viewpoints and quiet corners

Prague’s green spaces provide a breather between sights. **Letná Park** sits on a hill above the centre, delivering a panoramic view that sweeps the river, the castle and the Old Town spires. The park also houses a large metronome that once marked the spot of a giant Stalin statue.

Further south, **Petřín Hill** is reachable via a short funicular ride or a brisk walk. At the summit, the **Petřín Lookout Tower** resembles a small Eiffel Tower and offers an unobstructed vista of the city’s rooftops.

**Vyšehrad**, a historic fort on a hill across the river, blends ancient walls with a peaceful cemetery and a sweeping view of the Vltava. All three spots are easy to reach on foot or with a quick tram ride.

**Key stops**:
- Letná Park
- Petřín Hill and Lookout Tower
- Vyšehrad

## Practical tips for getting around

Prague’s public transport – trams, metro and buses – is reliable and cheap. A single ticket works across all modes and can be bought from vending machines or a contactless card. Most attractions listed above sit within a 10‑20‑minute walk, so you’ll find yourself alternating between tram rides and short strolls.

The best time to visit is late spring or early autumn, when the weather is mild and the crowds thinner than in peak summer. Pack sturdy shoes for cobblestones, a compact rain‑proof jacket, and a reusable water bottle – the city’s public fountains are clean and free.
