We love a good walk through a city built on stone, story and a fair bit of wind. Edinburgh’s compact centre lets you tick off the best things to do in Edinburgh without hopping on a bus.

In this guide we’ll point out the landmarks, parks and cultural spots a printed guidebook would list, then finish with the practical bits – how to get around, when to visit and what to pack. All of it can be done on foot, at a pace that lets you stop for a photo or a quick coffee whenever you feel like it.

## Historic heart: the Royal Mile

The Royal Mile runs from the foot of Edinburgh Castle down to Holyrood Palace, but it’s really a string of streets, each with its own flavour. As you head up you’ll pass St Giles’ Cathedral, a medieval church still holding occasional services, and the historic Parliament House, where the Scottish Parliament met for centuries.

The climb up Castle Rock is short but steep; once you’re on top the city spreads out below, a satisfying payoff for every step.

On the way down you’ll wander past the busy Grassmarket, a historic square now alive with street performers and bakeries offering fresh scones.

## Top things to do in Edinburgh on foot

- **Climb Arthur’s Seat** – the ancient volcano in Holyrood Park rewards you with a panoramic city view and a glimpse of the Firth of Forth. The path is well‑marked and suitable for most walkers.
- **Explore the Palace of Holyroodhouse** – the monarch’s official Scottish residence, with state apartments and the ruins of a 12th‑century abbey open to the public.
- **Wander through the Georgian New Town** – a grid of elegant squares, wide streets and neoclassical facades. Charlotte Square and George Street showcase 18th‑century ambition.
- **Visit the National Museum of Scotland** – free entry and a mix of natural history, art and technology. The rooftop terrace offers another city vista.
- **Stroll along the Water of Leith** – a riverside walk that takes you past leafy bridges, historic mills and the charming Dean Village.

Each stop links naturally to the next, giving you a solid taste of Edinburgh without a car.

## Castles and viewpoints

Edinburgh Castle dominates the skyline and is usually the first stop for walkers. Its stone walls house the Crown Jewels, the 16th‑century Great Hall and the National War Museum. Even if you skip the interior, the outer ramparts provide a clear line of sight over the rooftops.

A short climb from the main gate leads to the ruins of St Margaret’s Chapel, perched on the castle rock’s edge. The spot feels quiet, as if the city’s bustle is miles away.

For a less crowded perspective, head to Calton Hill. Its gentle slope brings you to the National Monument and the Nelson Monument, both offering sweeping views of the Old Town, the Firth and, on a clear day, the Pentland hills.

All three viewpoints sit within comfortable walking distance, making a satisfying half‑day loop.

## Parks and green spaces

Holyrood Park surrounds Arthur’s Seat and feels like wilderness in the city centre. Besides the summit, gentle paths wind around Duddingston Loch, where swans glide and the occasional bagpiper’s tune drifts over the water.

The Royal Botanic Garden is reachable on foot if you enjoy a longer stroll along the Water of Leith. Its glasshouses showcase tropical plants, while the open lawns invite a sunny picnic.

For a more urban green space, head to Princes Street Gardens. Tucked between the Old and New Towns, these gardens provide a flat, shaded route linking the castle with the Scott Monument, and they host seasonal flower displays that brighten the stone surroundings.

## Museums, galleries and markets

Beyond the National Museum, Edinburgh offers a handful of specialised venues. The Scottish National Gallery on the Mound houses works from the Renaissance to the early 20th century, while the nearby Scottish National Portrait Gallery celebrates notable Scots in a striking modern building.

If contemporary art is your thing, the Fruitmarket Gallery in the Old Town presents rotating exhibitions in a converted grain market. Admission is free for most shows, and workshops often run without booking.

Market lovers should wander to the Grassmarket on a Saturday morning. Open‑air stalls sell everything from local crafts to fresh produce, and a nearby bakery offers a warm roll perfect for a quick snack.

All these cultural spots sit within walking distance, letting you curate your own mini‑circuit of art and history.

## Practical tips – getting around, best time to visit, what to bring

Edinburgh’s city centre is compact, so most attractions are reachable on foot. If you need to cross the River Forth, the pedestrian bridges at North and South Bridge are safe and well‑signposted. Buses run frequently, but a short walk often saves you a fare and reveals hidden courtyards.

Late spring to early autumn offers the longest daylight and generally mild weather. Summer can be busy, especially during the festival period, so book accommodation early if you plan to stay overnight.

Pack a waterproof jacket – Edinburgh weather changes quickly. Comfortable shoes, a reusable water bottle and a map (or a smartphone with our browser‑based TourHunts puzzles for a quick brain teaser) will keep you comfortable.

We hope this guide gives you a clear route for a relaxed day of exploring Edinburgh on foot. Happy walking!
