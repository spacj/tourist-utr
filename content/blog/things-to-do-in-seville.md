We love strolling through Seville because the city folds history, colour and rhythm into a single, walkable canvas. In this guide we list the best things to do in Seville on foot, keeping your feet moving and curiosity switched on.

From monumental churches to riverside streets, each stop is easy to reach on foot and offers a glimpse of why this Andalusian capital feels like a living museum. Grab a comfortable pair of shoes, a bottle of water and let us lead you through the highlights.

## The Cathedral and Giralda: soaring history

Seville Cathedral dominates the skyline and claims the title of the world’s largest Gothic cathedral. Its nave stretches on seemingly forever, while chapels hide priceless artworks. A short climb up the Giralda bell tower rewards you with a panoramic sweep of red‑tiled roofs and the river.

Take the ramped steps rather than the spiral stairs – they’re kinder to the calves and let you savour each level’s details. Inside, don’t miss the tomb of Christopher Columbus and the intricate choir stalls; they’re easy to overlook in a rush.

## Alcázar Palace: Moorish rooms and gardens

The Real Alcázar is a palace that grew over centuries, mixing Islamic, Gothic and Renaissance touches. Mudéjar rooms glitter with geometric tiles that shift in the light, and the central courtyard with its fountain invites a brief pause.

Beyond the rooms, the gardens sprawl like a maze of orange trees, fountains and tiled paths. Follow the shaded lanes to discover quiet alcoves that frame views of the city beyond the walls. Tickets are reasonably priced and the experience feels worth it.

## Plaza de España and Parque de María Luisa: open‑air grandeur

Plaza de España, built for the Ibero‑American Exposition of 1929, feels like a set piece from a film. Its semi‑circular layout is lined with tiled alcoves representing each Spanish province, and you can hire a rowboat to glide along the surrounding canal – a surprisingly relaxing way to admire the decorative bridges.

Just behind the square, Parque de María Luisa spreads out with fountains, statues and shaded avenues. It’s perfect for a leisurely stroll after the plaza’s grandeur. Keep an eye on the ornate Fountain of the Lions, a favourite photo spot that never feels overrun.

## Triana neighbourhood and Mercado de Triana: riverside rhythm

Across the Guadalquivir, the Triana district offers a lively contrast to the historic centre. Narrow streets are packed with ceramic workshops where artisans still hand‑paint tiles in traditional patterns, giving the neighbourhood a vibrant, relaxed feel, especially on a Saturday morning.

A short walk brings you to Mercado de Triana, a covered market brimming with fresh fruit, olives and local cheeses. We often linger at a nearby family‑run bakery for toasted bread with jamón. The riverbank promenade then provides a scenic route back towards the city centre, with the Torre del Oro shimmering at sunset.

## Top things to do in Seville on foot

* Wander the Santa Cruz quarter, winding through white‑washed houses and secret courtyards.
* Stop at the Metropol Parasol for a modern contrast and a quick lift to its rooftop walkway.
* Visit the Museum of Fine Arts to see works by Murillo and Zurbarán – entry is modestly priced.
* Take a detour to the Archivo de Indias, where the archives of the Spanish empire are stored.
* End the day with a stroll along the river, watching the lights reflect on the water.

## Practical tips: getting around, best time to visit and what to pack

We find the city centre compact enough to explore on foot, but the tram and bus network are handy when you need a breather. Single tickets or day passes are available from any kiosk and are reasonably priced.

The most pleasant months for walking run from late March to early June and again from September to October, when the heat is gentle. Pack a lightweight hat, sunscreen, a reusable water bottle and sturdy shoes – the cobbles can be uneven.

Finally, stay aware of pickpockets in crowded areas and keep your valuables close. A quick stop for a cold drink in a plaza can be the perfect way to recharge before the next leg of your walk.
