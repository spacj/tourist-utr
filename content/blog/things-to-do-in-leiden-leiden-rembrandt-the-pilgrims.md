## Things to do in Leiden on a single afternoon

Leiden’s compact layout lets you stroll from the busy market square to the tranquil canals without breaking stride. The 17th‑century streets are lined with gabled houses, each hinting at a story you can spot in a glance. By following the TourHunts route you get a tidy framework that keeps the pace relaxed.

Because the route runs entirely in your browser, there’s nothing to install. Open the link on your phone, tap the map, and let the riddles point the way. The first hunt is free; any additional hunts are €5 each, so you can keep exploring without breaking the bank.

## Rembrandt’s birthplace and the art heritage

The walk opens at the house where Rembrandt Harmenszoon van Rijn entered the world in 1606. Today the Rembrandt House Museum occupies the site, but even the façade alone tells a tale – the narrow front, stepped gable and modest shop front are classic Leiden merchant architecture.

Seeing the spot where a master grew up adds a tactile layer to the canvases you may have admired from afar. It also underlines how Leiden was a busy hub of trade and culture, a vibe that still echoes through the city’s galleries and public art.

## The Pilgrim Fathers and the Pieterskerk

A short walk from Rembrandt’s house brings you to the Pieterskerk, a Gothic church that hosted a quiet but pivotal moment in trans‑Atlantic history. In the early 1600s a group of English Separatists gathered here to pray before boarding the Mayflower.

The soaring spire dominates the skyline, giving you a reliable landmark among the winding canals. Inside, the vaulted ceiling and plain wooden pews convey the solemn atmosphere the Pilgrims would have known – a stark contrast to the busy streets outside.

## Leiden University: the cradle of Dutch scholarship

Leiden University, founded in 1575, is the Netherlands’ oldest university and still ranks among Europe’s most respected. Its red‑brick buildings sit beside the river, their sober lines set against the older timber houses.

Passing the historic lecture halls, you can picture scholars such as Hugo Grotius or Carl Linnaeus debating ideas that still shape modern thought. The campus injects a lively academic buzz into the city; you’ll often hear conversations in several languages spilling out of courtyards.

## Tulips at Hortus Botanicus: Europe’s first bloom

The final stop is the Hortus Botanicus, one of the world’s oldest botanical gardens. In the early 1600s it was the first place in Europe where tulips were cultivated, sparking the infamous “tulip mania”.

Even outside peak season the historic glasshouses and neatly arranged beds showcase plants tended for centuries. A leisurely stroll through the pathways reveals the careful layout that reflects the scientific curiosity of the university’s early botanists.

## Practical tips for the walk

- **Getting there**: Leiden is well linked by train from Amsterdam, The Hague and Utrecht; the main station is a short walk from the centre.
- **Best time to go**: Late spring or early autumn offers mild weather and fewer crowds, making the riddles easier to solve on foot.
- **What to bring**: A charged smartphone, comfortable shoes and a light rain jacket – Dutch weather can change quickly.
- **Food and drink**: You’ll find plenty of bakeries and cafés around the market square for a quick bite.
- **After the walk**: If you enjoyed the experience, try another TourHunts route; the first hunt is free, and each extra hunt costs €5.

With these pointers in mind, you’re set for an afternoon that blends art, history and a touch of botanical intrigue – all without a guide, just a clever puzzle and your own curiosity.
