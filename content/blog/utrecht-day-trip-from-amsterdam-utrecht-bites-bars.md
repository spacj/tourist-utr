We start the day at Amsterdam Centraal and hop on a train that whisks us north to Utrecht in under half an hour. The ride is cheap, the scenery pleasant, and the city’s compact centre means we can walk everywhere without breaking a sweat.

Once we step off at Utrecht Centraal, the cobbled streets and the towering Dom already hint at why the Dutch love this place. Our plan centres on the "Utrecht Bites & Bars" self‑guided crawl – five stops, about 2.2 km, and roughly three hours of sipping, snacking and spotting the city’s quirkiest corners.

## Getting there – Utrecht day trip from Amsterdam made easy

The quickest way to start a Utrecht day trip from Amsterdam is the regular train. Services run every fifteen minutes and the trip takes around 25 minutes. Tickets are inexpensive and can be sorted in the NS app before you leave the house.

We recommend buying a return ticket so you can relax on the way back without hunting for a machine. The station sits right beside the city’s main canal, the Oudegracht, so you’re already in the middle of the action when the doors open.

## From Utrecht Centraal to the first stop of the crawl

Utrecht Centraal is a modern hub framed by historic brick facades. Exit onto the busy pedestrian zone and turn left onto the Oudegracht. The first stop of the "Utrecht Bites & Bars" route is a café tucked behind a row of canal boats – the kind of place where you can watch the water flow while nursing a coffee.

The self‑guided walk is browser‑based, so you simply open the TourHunts page on your phone, tap “Start”, and the map guides you step by step. The first hunt is free, and if you enjoy the rest of the crawl you can unlock it for €5.

## A bar inside a former church – sacred sipping

Our second stop is perhaps the most striking: a bar set within the vaulted walls of a former church. The high arches and stained‑glass windows give the space a reverent atmosphere, even as you order a craft lager or a gin tonic.

We like to linger here, because the acoustics turn even a quiet conversation into something a bit theatrical. The bar serves a selection of local brews, and the staff are happy to point out the historical details you might otherwise miss.

## The medieval castle that brews its own beer

The third stop brings us to a medieval city castle that now houses a brewery. The stone walls, turreted roofs and a small courtyard create a cosy backdrop for tasting house‑made ale.

We usually join the short tour that explains how the beer is brewed on‑site, then settle at a wooden table to sample the flagship pint. The castle’s cellar keeps the beer at just the right temperature, and the view over the surrounding rooftops is a pleasant bonus.

## Terrace where the canal ends – a perfect finish

The fourth and final stop of the crawl is a terrace that literally marks the end of a canal. Here the Oudegracht opens onto a small square, and the terrace offers a relaxed spot to watch boats drift by.

We recommend ordering a light snack – perhaps a cheese plate or a seasonal sandwich – and soaking up the late‑afternoon light. From this point the walk loops back towards the centre, allowing us to see the Dom Tower silhouetted against the sky as we head back to the station.

## Practical tips for a smooth Utrecht day trip

**Best time:** Mid‑morning to early afternoon gives us enough daylight for the crawl and avoids the rush‑hour crowds at the train station.

**What to bring:** Comfortable shoes, a charged phone for the browser‑based TourHunts map, and a light jacket – Utrecht can be breezy by the water.

**Getting back:** Trains from Utrecht Centraal to Amsterdam run frequently until late, so you can choose a convenient return.

**Cost:** The first hunt on the "Utrecht Bites & Bars" route is free; any additional hunts are €5 each. Everything else – train tickets and drinks – stays inexpensive.
