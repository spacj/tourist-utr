We start the day at Amsterdam Centraal, grab a cheap NS ticket and hop on the half‑hourly train to Utrecht. The ride is about half an hour, the scenery is mostly flat fields and a few windmills, and the stations are well sign‑posted, so you’ll feel confident even if it’s your first time. Once you step off at Utrecht Centraal you’ll be right in the city’s centre – a short walk brings you to the busy Oudegracht and the towering Dom.

From there we suggest the self‑guided “Utrecht Bites & Bars” walk. It’s a 2.2 km route that takes about three hours, with five stops that showcase the city’s love of coffee, craft beer and water‑front ambience. The first hunt is free, then it costs €5, and you run it straight from your browser – no download needed.

## Getting there: Utrecht day trip from Amsterdam by train

The NS service between Amsterdam Centraal and Utrecht Centraal runs every 15 minutes on weekdays and every 30 minutes on weekends. Trains are inexpensive and you can check exact times on the NS app. When you arrive, follow the signs to the main hall; the city’s pedestrian network radiates from the station, so you’ll quickly find the Oudegracht. If you prefer a tram, line 12 stops a few minutes’ walk from the Dom and connects nicely with the start of our walk.

## First stop: coffee in a former church

Our first cue on the “Utrecht Bites & Bars” route leads you to a café tucked inside a former church. The high vaulted ceiling and stained‑glass windows give the space a quiet grandeur, while the barista serves a solid espresso that will wake you up better than any alarm. We like to linger here for a few minutes, soaking up the light and planning the rest of the day. **Tip:** ask for the house blend – it’s roasted locally and surprisingly smooth.

## Midday pause at the castle brewery

A short walk brings you to the medieval city castle that now houses its own brewery. The stone walls are still visible, and the brewmasters explain how they use historic cellars to age their beer. You can sample a pint of the flagship ale, which has a hint of caramel from the old wood barrels. The setting feels like a scene from a fairy‑tale, except the only dragons are the foam‑capped glasses.

**What to try:** the seasonal brew – it changes with the weather, so you’ll always get something fresh.

## Canal‑side terrace for lunch

The third stop on the route is a terrace that marks the end of the Oudegracht canal. From here you have an uninterrupted view of the water, the old wharf houses and the Dom tower looming in the background. It’s an ideal spot to enjoy a light lunch – perhaps a sandwich and a soft drink from a nearby vendor – while watching boats glide by. The terrace has a relaxed vibe; you can sit for as long as you like without feeling rushed.

## Wrapping up: the Dom Tower and Oudegracht stroll

After the terrace, the walk guides you past the Dom Tower, Utrecht’s most recognisable landmark. Even if you don’t climb the tower, the surrounding square offers plenty of photo opportunities. Continue along the Oudegracht, where the historic wharves house quirky shops and occasional street musicians. The route loops back toward the station, giving you a chance to soak in the city’s atmosphere one last time before you head back to Amsterdam.

If you still have energy, we recommend a quick stop at a nearby café for a final cup of tea before the train.

## Practical details for your Utrecht day trip from Amsterdam

**Getting there:** train from Amsterdam Centraal to Utrecht Centraal; check the NS app for times and tickets.
**Best time:** late morning to early afternoon, so you can fit the three‑hour “Utrecht Bites & Bars” walk and still catch an evening train back.
**What to bring:** comfortable shoes, a light rain jacket (the Dutch weather can be unpredictable), and a fully charged device for the TourHunts route. The first hunt is free; subsequent hunts cost €5 and run straight in your browser.
**Extra tip:** the city centre is largely car‑free, so you’ll enjoy a pleasant, traffic‑free walk.
