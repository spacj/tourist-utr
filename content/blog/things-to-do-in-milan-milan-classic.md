Milan rewards a slower, curious pace. From the soaring spires of its cathedral to the quiet rows of Roman columns framing a lively aperitivo scene, the Lombardy capital crams centuries of art, design and street life into a walkable centre.

For an afternoon of exploring Milan, the *Milan Classic* puzzle walk from TourHunts delivers six essential stops, about 4.2 km of cobblestones and roughly 150 minutes of riddles that guide you from one landmark to the next. The first hunt is free; any further hunt costs €5, so you can try it without committing up front.

## Top things to do in Milan on foot

Walking is the simplest way to soak up Milan’s layered history. You’ll start near Italy’s largest cathedral – the Duomo – a Gothic masterpiece whose marble façade shifts colour with the light. From there the city’s grid opens up to reveal quiet courtyards, 19th‑century arcades and the sleek glass roof of the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II.

The *Milan Classic* route strings those highlights together without a guidebook. Each stop is marked by a riddle that points you toward the next landmark, turning a simple stroll into a light‑hearted scavenger hunt. By the end you’ll have traced a line that cuts across six centuries of Milanese life.

## Milan’s architectural layers

The Duomo dominates the skyline, yet the walk quickly moves beyond its stone grandeur. A short detour brings you to the historic Castello Sforzesco, a fortress whose courtyards house museums and frescoes. Its massive walls whisper stories of the Renaissance dukes who once ruled the city.

Further on, the elegant arches of the Brera district appear, where 18th‑century palazzos sit beside contemporary art galleries. The contrast between old and new recurs throughout Milan, and the puzzle walk pauses at each transition, giving you a moment to appreciate the city’s evolving identity.

## Savouring Milan’s aperitivo culture

One of the stops leads to a row of Roman columns that now host a lively aperitivo scene. Locals gather after work to sip vermouth and nibble on small plates. The tradition is simple: a modest drink buys you olives, cured meats and cheese, all served in a relaxed, outdoor setting.

Even if you don’t drink, the ambience merits a break. The columns provide a shaded perch where you can watch the city’s rhythm slow for an hour, while the scent of fresh bread from a nearby bakery drifts through the air.

## Mid‑day pauses and local flavours

Milan’s streets are dotted with family‑run bakeries, market stalls and modest trattorias. A quick bite of warm focaccia or a slice of panettone (if you happen to be there in winter) can recharge you before the next clue.

Because the walk is medium difficulty, you’ll move at a comfortable pace, leaving time to linger at a market or watch a street performer. The 4.2 km distance means you won’t be sprinting, but you’ll still get a decent workout – perfect for an afternoon that feels both active and relaxed.

## Practical details

**Getting there** – Milano Centrale links well to the airport and regional lines. A short tram or metro ride from the station drops you in the Duomo area, the natural starting point for the walk.

**Best time** – Late spring to early autumn offers pleasant temperatures and longer daylight. Mid‑week afternoons tend to be less crowded, giving you space to solve riddles without a rush.

**What to bring** – Comfortable shoes, a water bottle, and a fully charged phone (the TourHunts walk runs in the browser). A light jacket is useful for the Roman‑column aperitivo spot, where evenings can turn breezy.
