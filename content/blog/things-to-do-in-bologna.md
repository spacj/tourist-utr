We love a city that lets you wander from one historic square to the next without ever needing a map. Things to do in Bologna are best enjoyed on foot – the red‑brick roofs, endless porticoes and lively markets turn a simple stroll into a relaxed adventure, whether it’s your first visit or a return trip.

Grab a pair of comfortable shoes, a bottle of water and let’s set off, step by step, through the heart of this university town.

## Top things to do in Bologna on foot

Piazza Maggiore feels like the natural launch pad for any stroll. The open space is framed by the massive Basilica di San Petronio, its unfinished façade dominating the skyline. Across the square, the Fountain of Neptune adds a splash of baroque drama, while Palazzo d'Accursi houses the city hall and often hosts free exhibitions.

From there you can drift into the surrounding streets – Via dell'Indipendenza leads to elegant arcades, and a short detour brings you to the medieval Palazzo del Podestà, now a civic museum. All of this fits comfortably into a half‑hour walk, with plenty of chances to pause and soak up the atmosphere.

**Key points**
- Piazza Maggiore and its surrounding monuments
- Basilica di San Petronio (free to admire from the outside)
- Fountain of Neptune and Palazzo d'Accursi

## The Two Towers and the medieval streets

Spotting the Two Towers – the leaning Garisenda and the taller Asinelli – is a rite of passage for any walker in Bologna. The Asinelli Tower is open to the public; the climb is steep, but the panorama over the red‑tiled roofs rewards the effort. After descending, wander down Via Zamboni, the historic university street, where centuries‑old student buildings sit beside modern cafés.

The surrounding alleys, such as Via del Nettuno and Via del Pratello, are lined with stone façades and tiny courtyards that reveal the city’s medieval layout. Look up and you’ll see the characteristic mix of brick and terracotta that defines Bologna’s skyline.

**Highlights**
- Asinelli Tower climb (moderate effort)
- Garisenda Tower for a quick photo
- University quarter streets with lively student life

## Porticoes and the University quarter

Bologna boasts more than 38 kilometres of porticoes, turning a simple walk into a sheltered promenade. Starting from Piazza del Nettuno, follow the covered walkways eastward until you reach the Archiginnasio, the historic seat of the University of Bologna, the oldest university in the western world.

Even if you don’t step inside, the Archiginnasio’s exterior courtyard offers a quiet spot to rest. The surrounding area is peppered with bookshops, student stalls selling cheap snacks, and modest gardens that feel far removed from the city’s bustle.

**Don’t miss**
- The endless porticoes for shade on hot days
- Archiginnasio and its Anatomical Theatre
- Small gardens tucked behind university buildings

## Markets and food stalls

Bologna’s markets are a feast for the senses. The Mercato delle Erbe, housed in a glass‑capped building near the university, offers fresh produce, local cheeses and a selection of ready‑made dishes. A short walk away, the Quadrilatero district is a historic market street where stalls sell cured meats, fresh pasta and seasonal fruit.

Even without sitting down for a full meal, you can sample a slice of mortadella, a piece of freshly baked focaccia or a steaming cup of espresso from a family‑run bakery. The atmosphere is lively but not overwhelming, making it easy to pause, snack and continue your walk.

**Typical finds**
- Fresh mozzarella and regional cheeses
- Hand‑rolled pasta from a stall
- Sweet pastries from a local bakery

## Museums and art spaces

If you need a cultural break, Bologna’s museums sit conveniently within walking distance of the city centre. The Pinacoteca Nazionale houses a collection of Renaissance paintings, including works by the Carracci brothers. Nearby, MAMbo – the modern art museum – showcases contemporary Italian artists in a bright, airy setting.

For a deeper dive into the city’s past, the Museo della Storia di Bologna offers interactive displays that trace Bologna from its Roman roots to the present day. Entry fees are modest, keeping the museums accessible for most travellers.

**Suggested stops**
- Pinacoteca Nazionale for Renaissance art
- MAMbo for contemporary exhibitions
- Museo della Storia di Bologna for local history

## Practical tips for walking around Bologna

**Getting around** – The historic centre is compact and largely pedestrianised, so walking is the easiest way to explore. If you need to cover longer distances, the city’s bus network runs frequently and tickets can be bought at newsagents; a single ride is cheap.

**Best time to visit** – Late spring and early autumn bring mild temperatures and fewer crowds. Summer can be hot, but the porticoes provide shade, and the evenings are pleasant for a stroll.

**What to bring** – Comfortable shoes are essential; the cobblestones can be uneven. A reusable water bottle helps you stay hydrated, and a small umbrella is handy for sudden rain.

**Final note** – Bologna rewards a leisurely pace. Take your time, savour the aromas from market stalls, and let the city’s historic layers reveal themselves as you walk.
