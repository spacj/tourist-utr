## Things to do in Amsterdam on foot in an afternoon

Amsterdam’s compact centre lets you cover a lot without breaking a sweat, especially when you keep a steady pace. In just an afternoon the Amsterdam Underground walk squeezes 11.5 km into your itinerary, showing you corners most tourists never see. You’ll drift past the iconic canals, glide alongside the historic façades of the Jordaan, then slip into neighbourhoods where the city’s alternative spirit still hums.

Because the route is built around puzzles, you won’t just be strolling – you’ll be decoding clues that point you to the next spot. That means you’ll linger over a wall or a doorway longer than on a typical sightseeing tour, and you’ll come away with a sharper sense of place.

## Amsterdam Underground: squats and street art

One of the most striking stops is a squat that has simply refused to leave. Inside, a community has turned a neglected building into a lively home, its walls covered in murals that shout political messages and personal stories in equal measure. The art is raw, unpolished and unapologetically local – a perfect snapshot of Amsterdam’s restless counter‑culture.

From there the walk threads through alleys and underpasses where artists have turned concrete into colour. You’ll spot large‑scale pieces that nod to the city’s maritime past alongside tiny tags that only a keen eye catches. The mix of sanctioned murals and spontaneous graffiti keeps the experience feeling more like a treasure hunt than a guided tour.

## From a bunker under Vondelpark to a free ferry

The bunker hidden beneath Vondelpark is a highlight you won’t expect. While most visitors linger above ground, the puzzle leads you down into a wartime shelter now used for cultural events. Its stark concrete walls echo with history, a stark contrast to the park’s leafy serenity.

After you surface, the route takes you to a free ferry that shuttles across the water to a shipyard bristling with street art. The short ride offers a breather and a view of historic vessels and modern yachts. On the other side, massive murals cover hulls and warehouses, turning the industrial site into an open‑air gallery.

## What the walk entails: distance, time and difficulty

The Amsterdam Underground walk is rated hard, mainly because the riddles demand careful observation and a bit of lateral thinking. The total distance is about 11.5 km, which you’ll cover in roughly 240 minutes if you allow time for each clue. The terrain is flat overall, but you’ll navigate cobblestones, stairways into the bunker and the occasional canal bridge.

There are six stops, each marking a different facet of the city’s underground scene. Because the route is self‑paced, you can linger where you like – perhaps sketch a striking piece of street art or chat with locals in the squat. Remember, the difficulty refers to the mental challenge rather than a steep climb.

## Practical tips: getting there, best time, what to bring

**Getting there** – The walk starts in the centre, easy to reach by tram or bike from Amsterdam Central Station. A bike can get you close, but be prepared to walk once the puzzle begins.

**Best time** – Late spring to early autumn gives pleasant weather and long daylight hours, which helps when you’re solving clues outdoors. Mid‑week tends to be quieter, giving you space to examine the street art without crowds.

**What to bring** – A fully charged phone, comfortable shoes and a light rain jacket (Amsterdam weather is changeable). The first hunt is free, so you won’t need entry fees, but a few euros for a snack from a family‑run bakery near the market can be handy.
