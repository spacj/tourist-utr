Picture yourself beneath the Eiffel Tower or staring at the Colosseum – the moment is perfect until a pickpocket reaches into your bag. **How to avoid pickpockets** isn’t about living in fear; it’s about a handful of sensible habits that let you soak up the sights without a constant glance over your shoulder.

Below we unpack the tricks thieves rely on and hand you straightforward steps you can apply on any street, from Amsterdam’s canals to Rome’s piazzas. Whether you’re snapping a photo, checking a map, or chatting with a local, the advice stays the same.

## How to avoid pickpockets: understand the tactics

Distraction is a pickpocket’s favourite tool. In crowded spots they may spark a brief commotion – a street performer near the Rijksmuseum, a sudden announcement at a train station, or a friend bumping into you near the Trevi Fountain. While you’re looking away, the thief reaches for loose items: wallets, passports, or the phone dangling from a strap.

When you spot any of these signs, pause and check that nothing is missing before you move on.

## Keep your phone out of sight

Because your phone is small, valuable and often exposed, thieves gravitate towards it. Treat it like cash:

- **Store it in a zippered pocket** of your trousers or jacket, not in an open backpack.
- **Use a phone strap** that loops under your shirt or around your wrist, so it can’t be snatched in a quick grab.
- When you need directions, **pull it out briefly**, glance, then tuck it away again.

If you’re photographing the Anne Frank House, hold the phone at chest height and keep your other hand free to feel any extra pressure on your bag.

## Use secure pockets and bags

Not every pocket offers the same protection. Inside‑front pockets with zippers or button closures are far safer than outer slits. A small cross‑body bag that sits against your body, with the main compartment in front, adds an extra layer of defence.

- **Avoid back pockets** – they’re the first place thieves reach.
- **Close all zippers** before you walk down a busy street, such as the promenade by the canals in Utrecht.
- If you carry a larger bag, consider a **lockable luggage tag** that deters casual thieves.

These habits add a few seconds to a thief’s plan, often enough for you to notice and react.

## Stay aware in crowds at landmarks

Famous sites draw dense crowds, which is prime pickpocket territory. Simple awareness works best:

- **Walk on the periphery** of the crowd rather than in the centre where it’s hardest to see behind you.
- **Keep your bag in front of you**, especially in busy zones.
- **Limit the number of items** you carry. A slim wallet and a phone are enough; leave extra cards at your accommodation.

Positioning yourself slightly ahead of the flow reduces the chance of a quick lift.

## Quick checks before you sit or use transport

Transport hubs and cafés are common hunting grounds. Before you sit down on a bench near the Brandenburg Gate or board a tram, take a brief scan:

- **Secure your bag** on your lap with both hands, or place it on your feet with a strap over the back of the chair.
- **Confirm your phone is still in its pocket** before you pull out a ticket or a map.
- When you exit a bus or train, **pause for a moment** to make sure nothing has shifted or been removed.

A few seconds of attention can stop a theft before it happens.

## Key takeaways

- Pickpockets rely on distraction; recognise sudden crowd movements.
- Keep your phone hidden in a zippered or strapped pocket.
- Use front‑facing, zip‑closed pockets; avoid back pockets.
- Stay on the edge of crowds at landmarks and keep bags in front of you.
- Perform a brief check before you sit, stand, or board transport.

Adopting these habits doesn’t require a security guard – just a moment of attention. Your phone, passport, and wallet will stay safe, letting you focus on the sights rather than the risk.
