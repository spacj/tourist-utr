We’ve spent a few days wandering Florence’s stone‑streets, and we can point out which corners deserve a linger and which you can breeze past. In this guide we focus on the things to do in Florence that give you a real feel for the city without getting stuck in endless queues. Walk where the locals walk, pause at the places that have stood for centuries, and keep an eye out for the quieter lanes that reveal the city’s character – all on foot, with a map or a good pair of headphones for a self‑guided audio cue.

## Things to do in Florence: the historic centre and the Duomo

The cathedral complex dominates the heart of the city. Brunelleschi’s red‑brick dome looms over everything; climbing it is a decent workout, but the panorama of rooftops rewards the effort. Opposite the dome, the Baptistery’s bronze doors sit in a quieter setting – the acoustics make even a whispered prayer feel grand.

Just a stone’s throw away, Giotto’s Campanile offers another climb for a slightly different outlook, or you can simply admire its intricate façade from Piazza del Duomo. The square is perfect for a coffee at a family‑run kiosk while the city wakes up.

## Art treasures: Uffizi and Accademia

For world‑class art without the crush of a packed museum, aim for a mid‑morning slot. The Uffizi still feels like a Renaissance palace, housing Botticelli, Michelangelo and Caravaggio. Take your time in the Tribuna; the natural light is a photographer’s delight.

A short walk brings you to the Accademia, home to Michelangelo’s David. Standing close enough to see the marble’s details can feel almost surreal. Both museums cost a reasonable ticket, but the experience is priceless.

## Riverside stroll: Ponte Vecchio and Oltrarno

Cross the Arno on the Ponte Vecchio, the medieval bridge lined with gold‑smiths’ workshops. Even if you’re not buying jewellery, the bridge buzzes with life and offers a lovely river view.

On the opposite bank, the Oltrarno feels lived‑in rather than tourist‑packed. Wander down Via de’ Benci and Via Santo Spirito, where artisan workshops, small galleries and quiet squares sit side by side. The gentle slope leads you toward the Pitti Palace and its expansive Boboli Gardens, which we’ll cover next.

## Markets and squares: San Lorenzo and Mercato Centrale

Florence’s market scene centres on historic San Lorenzo. The open‑air market in Piazza San Lorenzo sells everything from leather goods to fresh produce – a solid spot for a souvenir without the pressure of a shop.

Just a few steps away, Mercato Centrale offers a covered space where locals serve Tuscan staples. Grab a slice of focaccia, a bite of pecorino, or a glass of Chianti and soak up the lively yet relaxed atmosphere.

## Green escapes: Boboli Gardens and the Rose Garden

Behind the Pitti Palace, the Boboli Gardens spread out with statues, fountains and hidden nooks. Follow the winding paths up to the Forte di Belvedere for a panoramic view of terracotta roofs – a pleasant way to escape the heat on a sunny afternoon.

A short walk downhill lies the Rose Garden (Giardino delle Rose). This smaller park brims with fragrant roses and a terrace that looks out over the city. It’s an ideal spot to rest after a day of walking, and entry is free, making it a cheap but rewarding stop.

## Practical tips for a smooth Florence walk

**Getting around** – The historic centre is compact enough to explore on foot. If you need to go further, the tram and bus network runs frequently; tickets are available at kiosks or via contactless card.

**Best time to visit** – Late spring or early autumn bring mild weather and fewer crowds. Mid‑day can be warm, so slot indoor activities for those hours.

**What to bring** – Comfortable shoes, a refillable water bottle and a light scarf for sudden temperature changes. A portable charger keeps your phone ready for maps or audio cues.
