## Why an afternoon walk works best

Mid‑day light in Amsterdam is bright enough for clear photos without the harsh glare you get later. The compact centre lets you see a lot without wearing yourself out, and the breeze off the water keeps things comfortable.

A puzzle walk forces you to pause at each stop. You’ll spend a few minutes solving a cipher, then you have time to look around before moving on. It feels less rushed than a timed tour and more engaging than a casual wander.

## Royal Palace and Dam Square

Your first stop is the **Royal Palace** on Dam Square, a 17th‑century building that now hosts official functions. Its façade serves as a canvas for a cipher that nods to the Dutch Golden Age. While you’re there, glance up at the National Monument and watch the street performers – they add a lively backdrop to the puzzle.

Dam Square itself is a crossroads of history and modern life. From the palace you can see busy tram lines, cyclists and the occasional pigeon‑watcher. Solving the riddle here points you toward the next cultural hotspot.

## Van Gogh Museum and the art trail

The next major landmark is the **Van Gogh Museum**. Its collection of the painter’s most famous works makes it an obvious stop, but the puzzle we’ve placed focuses on a lesser‑known sketch that reveals a hidden detail about his technique.

Even if you skip the interior, the museum’s striking modern façade offers a perfect spot for a quick photo and a moment to ponder how the city celebrates its artistic heritage. The clue you solve will steer you onto the canal ring, where the next myth awaits.

## Things to do in Amsterdam on foot

After the museum, the walk follows the **Canal Ring**, a UNESCO‑listed network of waterways lined with narrow houses and leafy trees. One of the riddles challenges the common belief that all Amsterdam canals were built in the 17th century; the answer points to a later expansion many tourists miss.

You’ll also drift past the **Jordaan** neighbourhood, famous for its narrow lanes and tucked‑away courtyards. Here the puzzle asks you to spot a particular house number that once belonged to a celebrated writer – a tiny detail that rewards careful observation.

## Practical tips

**Getting there** – The start point is a short walk from Amsterdam Central Station, so you can hop off the train and head straight onto the route.

**Best time** – Late spring to early autumn offers pleasant weather and longer daylight. Mid‑week tends to be quieter than weekends, giving you space to think through the clues.

**What to bring** – A fully charged phone (the hunt works in any browser), comfortable shoes, and a light rain jacket just in case. A reusable water bottle is handy, and a small notebook can help you jot down answers.
